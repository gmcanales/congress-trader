TL;DR

  • v1 (run #1, filing_date anchor, grid over min_members=1,2,3): no edge. In-sample grid winners went OOS-negative — classic small-sample overfit.
  • Re-anchored on transaction_date (run #2): same conclusion at the same grid. Visible NAV freezes through 2025-Q1, Aug, Oct–Nov, and Feb 2026 came from the conviction filter eliminating 80–95% of buys plus the hard 21-day exit (every position auto-closes on a timer, so quiet stretches leave the strategy fully cashed-out).
  • Removing the conviction filter (runs #3 and #5, min_members=1, transaction_date) eliminated the freezes and appeared to yield +2.53% over SPY and +5.44% over RSP at OOS. Promising — but anchored on a date no follower can know in real time.
  • Actionable check (runs #7/#8): the edge collapses. Repeating the same strategy on filing_date — the only anchor that respects the up-to-45-day STOCK Act reporting lag — OOS strategy return drops from +20.44% to +15.08%. Excess vs SPY becomes −2.82%, and excess vs RSP collapses to +0.09% (effectively flat against equal-weight). The 5.4-point gap between transaction_date and filing_date is a real phenomenon — congressional buys are concentrated in names that drift up during the disclosure window — but the lag eats the alpha. A follower trading at the disclosure cannot capture it.
  • Final v1 conclusion stands: there is no actionable edge in broad congressional trading. The aggregated transaction_date excess in runs #3–#6 was look-ahead bias from anchoring on a date that isn't publicly observable in real time, not a strategy result.
  • Where to look next: theory-driven subgroup cuts (leadership / committee chairs per Wei & Zhou 2025) and exit-policy variants that may shift the picture beyond the timer-based event study used here. See "next steps".

Leadership-only result — narrow vs expanded universe

Runs #9 / #10 applied the leadership filter to only the narrow chamber-leadership universe (Speaker, Majority/Minority Leader, Whips, Conference Chairs) — 37 position tenures, 67 train trades, dominated by five members. Runs #11 / #12 repeat the same backtest after ingesting committee chairs and ranking members of the 117th–119th Congresses (157 position tenures, 460 train trades, much more distributed). Hold-period term structure (runs #11-#16)

The leadership runs above were all pinned at hold=63d. Runs #13/#14 (hold=21d) and #15/#16 (hold=126d) hold the universe and entry rule constant but vary the timer-exit. Two reasons not to interpret this as a grid search: (a) the per-trade event-study t-stats below are constant across runs because they measure trade-anchored forward returns independent of the hold (so the rules backtest is just catching different slices of the same per-trade curve), and (b) train excess is negative at every horizon, so picking the highest OOS number reintroduces the v1 overfit-by-selection trap. Data inputs

Source House Clerk PTRs (PDF) + Senate eFD (HTML). Ingestion via services/scraper.py, services/senate_scraper.py, and services/ptr_parser.py (text + OCR fallback).
Confidence filter All transactions included regardless of parser confidence (high, medium, low).
Asset universe Stock transactions only — rows with a non-empty ticker. Bonds, treasuries, mutual funds, options, and crypto are excluded.
Owner Member, spouse, and dependent trades all treated equally.
Position amount Not modeled. Congressional disclosures publish amount ranges ($1k–$15k, $15k–$50k, …) but the engine does not size positions on them.
Known data gap ~12% of House PTRs are scanned checkbox-grid forms that OCR poorly — deferred for v1. v1 results may under-represent those filings.

Backtest mechanics

Event anchor filing_date (the public date — tradeable; up to 45 days after the actual trade) or transaction_date (the date the member actually traded — not tradeable in real time; reported for diagnostics only). Each run states which it used.
Position entry The first trading day strictly after the event date.
Exit rule Hard timer. Every position closes after horizon_days trading days. There is no stop-loss, no profit target, no signal-based exit (e.g., closing when the same member sells). This is an event-study construct; it is not how a real strategy would behave.
Position sizing Equal-weight across all currently-open positions, rebalanced daily. Implicitly assumes fractional shares and zero rebalancing cost.
Conviction filter Optional min_members: a ticker qualifies only if it has ≥N distinct congressional buyers within a trailing 30-day window. The window length is hard-coded at 30 days in this version of the engine.
Benchmark Per-run. SPY (cap-weighted S&P 500) is the default; RSP (Invesco equal-weight S&P 500) is used as a cross-check on whether any SPY-relative excess is mechanical (equal-weight basis) vs informational (congressional signal). The benchmark choice affects in-sample grid selection — the "best" config on training data can shift between hold_days = 21 and 63 depending on benchmark, so cross-benchmark numbers are not strictly apples-to-apples unless the grid is held fixed.
Transaction costs Zero. No slippage, no spread, no commissions modeled.
Train / test split Single split. No walk-forward, no k-fold. Grid is searched on the training window; the held-out test window is touched exactly once with parameters locked from training.
Event-study horizons 5, 21, and 63 trading days forward from the event. Excess return = ticker return − SPY return over the same window.
Outcome buckets ±2% vs benchmark = outperform / in-line / underperform.

Side modeled

The rules backtest opens positions on purchases only. Sales feed the event study but do not drive position-opening. Short-selling is not modeled.

NAV — strategies vs SPY

Diagnosis: where the NAV "freezes" come from

Run #2 (min_members=2) shows visible flat stretches across 2025-01–03, all of 2025-08, 2025-10–11, and 2026-02. These are not data gaps — raw monthly purchase counts in 2025 are stable (50–80 buys per month). They are entirely produced by two mechanics already documented in the assumptions above:

  1. The conviction filter kills 80–95% of raw buys. Most congressional purchases are solo — one member, one ticker, no other member buying the same name within 30 days. Those events fail the min_members=2 threshold and never become positions. The chart below makes this concrete: light bars = raw purchases that month, dark bars = events that survive the filter.
  2. Every position auto-closes after horizon_days. The selected hold for run #2 was 21 trading days (~1 calendar month). When no new qualifying event arrives within 21 days of the prior one, every open position has expired by the time the gap is half over. With zero open positions n_open=0 ⇒ daily return = 0 ⇒ NAV is flat. This is a methodological artifact, not a bug or a market signal — but it does mean the strategy literally cannot stay invested through quiet stretches.

A position opened just before a gap is still moving with the market for ~21 trading days. The NAV becomes flat only once every pre-gap position has hit its hard horizon. Removing the conviction filter (run #3, min_members=1) raises invested-day coverage from 90% to 95% and eliminates the visible flat stretches.

Open questions & next steps

  1. Tradeable-anchor check — closed (runs #7/#8). Repeating the pinned grid on filing_date drops OOS excess to −2.82% (SPY) and +0.09% (RSP). The apparent transaction_date excess was look-ahead bias from anchoring on a date that isn't publicly observable in real time. Confirms that any future strategy claim must be reported on filing_date to be considered actionable.
  2. Signal-driven exits. The hard-timer exit is an event-study construct, not a strategy. A realistic strategy mixes new entries on buys, continued holds while no sale is disclosed, and active exits when the disclosing member sells (or a trailing/time-stop fires). Worth implementing, but be sober about the prior: if the broad-buy signal has no filing_date alpha, longer holds won't create one — exit-policy gains would have to come from the sell-side signal (event-study t-stats on sales are also sub-1 in this dataset, so this is a "test it cheaply, don't expect much" item).
  3. Leadership restriction (v2). Per the Wei & Zhou (NBER, 2025) finding, the rank-and-file null we see here is consistent with the literature; their identified +47pp/yr alpha is concentrated in members who hold leadership/committee-chair positions. Once positions tenure data is ingested, this same engine can be re-run on a leadership-only subset for a theory-driven cut.
  4. Conviction-window length. The 30-day clustering window is hard-coded. A sweep at cluster_days ∈ {30, 60, 90} may surface or rule out longer-tail clustering that the current min_members=2 setting misses.
