Context

This report supersedes the v1 alpha-hunting narrative. After Phase 1 of the strategy-framework work, two things became clear:

  1. The engine had a methodological bug through run #22: returns were reported at the calendar's extended tail (cal_end) rather than at test_end. This systematically biased numbers in favor of buy-and-hold benchmarks (by up to ~400 days of free drift for mirror_sell runs). Fixed starting with run #23. v2 only shows post-fix runs. The full historical narrative is preserved in generate_report.py v1 output.
  2. The leadership hypothesis cannot be cleanly resolved with our data, per the Bayesian model. The OOS excess on the strategy matrix below is captured by is_post_trifecta and sector composition, not leadership alpha. So this report is about which exit policy captures the most of the realized return honestly, not about claiming an alpha source.

Engine capabilities

The rules-backtest engine supports four exit policies. The recommendation system can dispatch to any of them based on a risk profile.

fixed_horizon Hard timer — every position closes after horizon_days. Event-study-shaped; useful as the methodological baseline but not a realistic strategy.
mirror_sell Close on the earliest of: same-member sale disclosure (filing_date), 252-day time stop fallback, or end-of-window. No trailing stop. Literal-follower strategy.
mirror_sell_with_trailing_stop Adds a fixed trailing_stop_pct (default 15%) trailing stop to mirror_sell. Risk-management layer; over-triggers in high-vol regimes.
mirror_sell_with_beta_trailing_stop As above, but with the stop scaled per-ticker by trailing-252d beta vs SPY: stop_pct × clip(β, 0.5, 3.0). Adaptive risk control — low-vol names get tight stops, high-beta names get wide ones.

What the data actually supports

The Bayesian hierarchical model (run #18, PyMC, NUTS sampling) regressed per-trade 63-day filing-date excess return on leadership_at_trade + is_post_trifecta + in_majority + party_R + sector dummies, with per-member random intercepts. Headline:

P(leadership effect > 0 | data, controls) = 0.582. Posterior mean is +0.25% per 63-day window with a 94% HDI of [-2.09%, +2.53%] — straddling zero. The rules-backtest "leadership edge" decomposes into a credibly-positive is_post_trifecta coefficient (+1.25%, P=0.90) and credibly- positive Energy sector (+3.7%, P=0.99). The leadership filter just happens to overlap with the trifecta window and the sector-tilted 2025 cohort.

Strategy choice in this report is about capturing more of the realized return with a defensible exit policy. The Bayesian result means we don't claim alpha beyond what the regressors above support.

Recommendation framework

The matrix above feeds the GET /api/recommendations endpoint. Risk profiles map to specific strategy configurations:

conservative Broad universe (all members), mirror+β stop. Risk-managed by per-ticker beta-scaled stop; widest universe; most diversified.
moderate Leadership universe, mirror+β stop. Narrower but risk-controlled. Falls between the two extremes.
aggressive Leadership universe, mirror_sell (no trailing stop). Highest historical OOS excess (+11.6% over SPY in 2025) but no intra-position drawdown protection; relies entirely on the literal-follower signal.

Risk-profile labels reflect the relative portfolio characteristics of each strategy, not a guarantee about future returns. Train/test instability persists across all configurations; the matrix above gives the empirical historical comparison.
